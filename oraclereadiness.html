The Oracle Readiness Screen

Eight questions to ask before you trust a test result from an automated or agentic test. Answer them about one real test, not about the tool in general. The first five are a gate. The last three are a vote.

Who decided correct here?

Part 1 · the gate

Where does “correct” come from?

Five questions. Any No stops the run.

Question 1 1 Can you state the expected result before the run? In writing, in plain words, before anything executes.
Question 2 2 Does it stay constant? The same definition of correct on the next run, and the one after that.
Question 3 3 Does it come from outside the code or the agent? The thing being judged should not be the one supplying the verdict.
Question 4 4 Can a human see what a self-heal changed? If the test adapted itself, a person can see what it adapted and judge whether it still means the same thing.
Question 5 5 Can a named person stop and redirect the run? A specific person, not “the team.”
Part 2 · the vote

Is it worth automating at all?

Three questions. Most Yes wins.

Question 6 6 Does it run more than once? A check you will repeat earns back the effort. A one-time check usually does not.
Question 7 7 Is the application stable? If it changes under you every week, you are maintaining the test, not using it.
Question 8 8 Does it reach conditions people can’t cover by hand? Volume, combinations, timing, or data that a person cannot realistically work through.

Your verdict

Answer the questions

Any No in Part 1 is a Stop. Otherwise Part 2 decides between Go and Hold.

Stop

Any No in Part 1. Nobody has said what correct means, or the thing being judged is judging itself. Fix that first.

Go

Part 1 is all Yes, and most of Part 2 is Yes. The result can be used as evidence, and it is worth automating.

Hold

Part 1 is all Yes, but most of Part 2 is No. The oracle is sound, but the case for automating it is not there yet.

Stop is a good answer.

A Stop that saves you from automating something with no oracle is the screen working.

Where it comes from. Part 2 is adapted from a classic automation checklist, where the rule was “Yes to most, automate.” The questions are a sampling of how I think about making and evaluation. I encourage adding your own as well. Part 1, and making it a gate and not a vote, is new.
