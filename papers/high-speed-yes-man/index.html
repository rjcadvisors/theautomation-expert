From High-Speed Idiot to High-Speed Yes-Man

Why AI speeds up output while organizations lose the ability to validate it

Rick Cavallaro · RJCadvisors Inc.

Download the full paper (PDF)
PDF · 21 pages · free, no sign-up

Abstract

Every technology before this one did what you told it. This one tells you what you want to hear. And when you push back, it argues.

For forty years, enterprise technology did what it was configured to do and failed in ways people could see and trace. This paper calls that the high-speed idiot. Generative and agentic AI is different. It interprets, extends and adapts, and the research increasingly shows it leans toward agreeing with the person using it. This paper calls that the high-speed yes-man.

The central argument is that AI accelerates output at the same time organizations may be weakening the human capacity, incentive systems and psychological conditions required to validate that output. Where that combination exists, the speed of the tool is a liability, not an advantage. The paper traces the pattern through eight layers, from the efficiency illusion to the validation bottleneck, tests it against documented organizational cases, and ends with practical ways to govern it.

Four terms the paper uses

  • High-speed idiot: classical deterministic automation, including scripts, RPA, rules-based workflows and conventional test automation. It executes exactly as written and fails visibly.
  • High-speed yes-man: a generative or agentic AI system that shows a measurable bias toward affirming the user’s stated position, and that interprets instructions and can act beyond them.
  • Persuasion bombing: the behavior reported in a BCG field study, in which a model answers an attempt to challenge its output by escalating its persuasive effort rather than reconsidering.
  • Human above the loop: a person with craft knowledge sets the criteria, boundaries, evidence standard and veto from outside the AI feedback cycle, rather than judging each output from within it.

What the paper covers

  • Why the economics of AI output and the economics of validation are moving in opposite directions.
  • The research on AI sycophancy and persuasion bombing, with the limits of each study stated plainly.
  • Documented cases where organizations moved faster than they could verify, and what happened.
  • Seven practices for governing the work, including resisting persuasion bombing.
  • Human above the loop: why the position of the human matters as much as the presence of one.

How to read it

The organizational failure model in this paper is my own practitioner synthesis. The cited studies support individual parts of it. They do not establish the whole. I offer it as a diagnostic lens for you to test against your own organization, and I would like to hear where it fits and where it does not.
